How the four components work

Scores are grade points out of 100—not probabilities. The worked examples above use the same rules below.

Lineup execution · 30%

The share of the best legal lineup's points captured by the submitted starters.

100 × actual points ÷ best legal lineup points

Roster & waiver management · 20%

First score opportunity capture, then adjust for moves and crowd-informed start/sit outcomes.

capture = 100 × actual ÷ (actual + qualified market gains) base = clamp(capture + 3 × move adjustment + win bonus, 45, 100) roster score = clamp(base + crowd adjustment, 45, 100)

Move adjustments are bounded to −6…+6; a move-assisted win adds 10 points. No qualifying upgrade means standing pat earns full opportunity capture.

Which waiver alternatives qualify?

The target must be a starter. Market gain compares the available player's points with the best same-position option already rostered, so existing bench value is not counted again as waiver value. At least three scoring samples, average points at least as high as the starter's, and a heuristic score ≥0.65 are required.

heuristic = min(1, 0.5 + min(gain, 20) ÷ 40)

This heuristic is not a probability. These are retrospective lineup alternatives, not recommendations to drop injured or benched players.

Move adjustment rules, in evaluation order

Surprise = completed points − prior scoring average. Sum the applicable per-addition adjustments and clamp the total to −6…+6.

Rules apply in order. Lineup impact currently uses a broad roster alternative; positional precision is a limitation.

All-play position · 25%

Compare the team with every other league team. A tie earns half a win. A 50% all-play rate maps to 75 grade points.

clamp(25 + all-play win percentage, 45, 100)

Scoring performance · 25%

The week's league-average score maps to 75. Each percentage point above or below that average changes the component by 2.5 points, before clipping.

clamp(75 + 2.5 × (100 × actual ÷ weekly league average − 100), 45, 100)

Crowd decision model

Alignment and quality answer different questions. Pearson correlation measures whether starter selection follows Yahoo starting popularity. It does not say whether those choices were good.

1. Set expectations before looking at results

Use the latest ownership and starting percentages strictly before the evaluated week. Missing rates are not zero. Exclude reserve slots, known byes and players with no recorded game. Correlation needs variation in both start/bench selection and starting rates.

2. Choose legal reference alternatives

Order starters by ascending start rate, then name. Match each with the highest-start-rate unused eligible bench player. Each bench player is used once. Flex accepts RB/WR/TE; fixed slots accept the starter's position. This deterministic reference is independent of points, but is not the globally optimal crowd lineup.

3. Score the outcome of disagreement

A pair is contrarian when the bench alternative's starting rate exceeds the starter's by at least 10 percentage points. Only those pairs enter the weighted outcome.

gap = (bench starting % − starter starting %) / 100 weight = gap × max(starter ownership %, bench ownership %) / 100 edge = actual starter points − bench alternative points weighted outcome = weight × tanh(edge / 8) crowd score = 75 + 25 × sum(weighted outcomes) / sum(weights) roster adjustment = 0.4 × (crowd score − 75)

Positive edges reward successful disagreement; negative edges penalize harmful disagreement. Ownership controls relative evidence weight; tanh bounds extreme outcomes. The adjustment is at most ±10 roster-component points, or ±2 final weekly grade points before clipping.

No covered contrarian choice means no adjustment—not a zero grade or zero roster-component weight. The lineup component still measures missed opportunities when following the crowd.

Season aggregation

Pool all covered weekly weights and outcomes through the report week, then use the same signed adjustment on the season transaction component. Do not average weekly crowd scores. Season alignment is the mean of defined weekly correlations; coverage is disclosed. Missing history is not reconstructed.

Definitions & limitations

clamp(x, 45, 100) keeps x between 45 and 100. tanh is a smooth curve bounded between −1 and +1. Weighted component contributions are summed to produce the final grade; displayed decimals may differ slightly because the calculation uses full precision.

This is retrospective outcome analysis, not a causal or predictive model. Historical injury designations are unavailable; bye-week metadata is available, but zero points alone never establish injury. Weekly ownership snapshots are not exact kickoff availability snapshots.

Component weights, scoring curves, clipping floors, move bonuses, the 10-point starting-rate gap, the 8-point outcome scale and the 0.4 crowd adjustment factor are modeling choices, not empirically calibrated probabilities.
