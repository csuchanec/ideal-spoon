Overall Manager Assessment

A retrospective rubric in grade points out of 100—not a probability or causal skill estimate.

Overall = .35 P + .35 E + .20 J + .10 S

Fixed shares: team performance 35%, lineup execution 35%, expectation-aware judgment 20%, roster stewardship 10%. Missing management evidence uses an explicit 75-point reference anchor at its unchanged share. Missing production makes the overall unavailable. Shares are never redistributed.

Season and historical assessments average compatible raw weekly overall observations and the same weekly component contributions, including declared anchors. They do not average letters or apply nonlinear formulas to averaged inputs. Sensitivity varies missing components over their stated ranges at fixed shares (judgment 50–100, other components 0–100); it is not a confidence interval.

P · Relative team performance

all-play = 100 × (wins + .5 × ties) / other league teams z = (points − weekly league mean) / weekly standard deviation scoring magnitude = 50 + 50 × tanh(z / 2) P = .5 × all-play + .5 × scoring magnitude

An equal-score cohort receives 50. This describes production; it is not the whole management assessment.

E · Legal lineup execution

R = max(0, supported optimal points O − actual points A) q = strongest feasible single-swap support: clip(positive bench points / 10, 0, 1) D = 10 × occupied lineup slot count E = clip(75 + 25 q − 100 R / D, 0, 100)

The denominator is a fixed per-slot scoring reference for the whole lineup: unrelated starter scoring cannot dilute a mistake, but a single contested position does not represent the entire lineup. Interchangeable decision slots, including FLEX/superflex chains, remain available as worked diagnostics. No alternatives means zero regret and no competition credit.

Retain actual starters, including zero/negative outcomes. Exclude reserves, known bench byes and hypothetical replacements without positive recorded participation. This is an evidence filter, not an inferred injury. No choice and a zero-point bench option both receive zero competition credit; playable scoring alternatives introduce continuous credit against a fixed 10-point reference, not an automatic 25-point jump. The credit is not divided by actual performance or summed across slots; stronger actual scoring cannot reduce it. Exact global legal regret remains the opportunity comparison. Participation does not prove availability at lineup lock.

J · Expectations and outcomes

C = legal reference lineup maximizing labeled expectation indices index = .8 × prior starting percentage + .2 × prior ownership percentage, when available fallbacks: .5 × prior ownership; 100 / (1 + positive draft ADP / 100); .5 × percent drafted last resort: recorded same-week starts, labeled actual deployment rather than a pre-week forecast U = sum(expectation indices) c = clip((U(C) − U(A)) / (100 × max(crowd replacement count, 1)), 0, 1) J_assessed = 75 + 25 c tanh((A − C) / 8) J_effective = 75 + expectation-reference coverage × (J_assessed − 75)

Prior starts remain the preferred reference; missing ownership uses starting percentage alone. Missing starts use prior ownership at half scale, then labeled imported draft ADP/demand references. If all are absent, recorded same-week starts provide a last-resort retrospective crowd-deployment comparison, not a pre-week forecast. These references do not invent a starting percentage or assert exact historical injury knowledge. Week 1 does not require imaginary Week 0 rates. Current-week post-result popularity is not substituted as a forecast. Only absence of every supported reference leaves judgment unassessed. Unknown actual starters remain held fixed; unknown bench expectations are excluded. Following the reference receives 75 even if a surprising bench breakout creates hindsight regret. Expectation gaps scale successful and costly dissent; these indices are rubric conventions, not scoring probabilities.

Judgment does not subtract hindsight regret a second time. Execution and judgment still share actual outcomes, so they are not independent evidence. Judgment's theoretical missing-evidence range is 50–100, symmetrically centered on 75; partial and historical point rankings require comparable coverage.

S · Roster stewardship

net acquisition = supported optimal after-roster points − supported optimal before-roster points acquisition score = 75 + 25 tanh(net acquisition / 8) no recorded moves = zero transaction effect = acquisition score 75 weighted gain = strongest single legal roster improvement × expectation index / 100 roster opportunity score = 85 − 35 tanh(weighted gain / 8) S = .5 × acquisition subscore + .5 × waiver subscore

Reconstruct successful transaction-linked adds/drops jointly, keeping occupied slots fixed. Signed legal roster-frontier value—not pickup bench points—is assessed retrospectively. Missing identities/scoring or inconsistent transactions remain genuine data gaps. Recorded zero from dropped nonparticipants is roster accounting, not a recommendation to start or drop an injured player. Known after-kickoff bench additions cannot earn expired current-week points; unresolved historical timing is disclosed separately rather than automatically halving the outcome.

No recorded moves is neutral transaction effect, not automatically good or bad management. Compare the observed unrostered pool with the best legal owned lineup, including bench options; missed bench points are not charged again. Exclude all league-rostered/transacted keys, nonparticipants, known byes and candidates without an expectation reference. Use one strongest expectation-weighted marginal improvement, not total free-agent points or summed mutually exclusive alternatives. Zero supported improvement scores 85 for comparative roster sufficiency; increasing weighted improvement smoothly lowers that score. This is an auction-claim league: unknown competing bids do not block scoring actual acquisition value or plausible missed upgrades. Recorded FAAB amounts are acquisition context; this weekly rule does not invent missing costs or add a budget-normalized ROI score. Historical injury knowledge is qualified separately, not treated as a blanket partial-coverage flag.
